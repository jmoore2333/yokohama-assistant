# Phase 3.5a — CLI redesign: design brief

**Date:** 2026-08-05 · **Status:** design brief, precedes the 3.5a spec · **Owner-directed** (rev 2 of the 3.5 scoping proposal): 3.5 is broken into phases; 3.5a is the CLI redesign, run as its own branch + PR, **closed by the live gate game successfully tracked start-to-finish on the redesigned surface**. A detailed handover to 3.5b (the strength lane) is written at this phase's close.

---

## 1. Why this phase exists — live-gate attempt 1, recorded

The owner began tracking a real game on the CLI as it stood (2026-08-05) and the experience was poor — the gate's own text is *"track one full physical game live, start to finish, **without fighting the tool**"*, and the tool was fought. The owner's findings, verbatim in substance:

- **"a lot of abbreviations"** — tile/goods/command shorthand everywhere the player needs plain words;
- **"it's very hard to see the game state"** — the composed frame does not read at a glance;
- **"how to track orders"** — no clear, continuously visible surface for orders (hands, ports, fulfillment progress);
- **"all the shortcuts required"** — too much memorized grammar (`a`/`b`/`m`/`r`, view names, sub-prompt syntax);
- **"the UI just isn't clean and clear"** — the overall design bar;
- **the frame is "unnecessarily small"** — the current layout crams into ≤140×~40 with no terminal-size use.

The gate is NOT claimed for attempt 1. These findings are the exposure map's CLI-usability class (ordinary CLI work, off-map — no gate or map amendment) and are this brief's requirements. Any **rules/mechanics** observations from the partial play are collected separately and route through the disposition at the 3.5a close (the essentials of the game were exercised, not every card — stated honestly in the handover).

## 2. Requirements (R1–R7)

**R1 — Plain words over abbreviations.** Full tile names, goods names, and command words wherever the player reads or types; abbreviations become *accepted aliases*, never the displayed form. The §8 language table (CLI vs rulebook vocabulary) stays the naming authority; the rulebook-facing term wins on screen.

**R2 — Game state at a glance.** The frame is reorganized so the standing questions of live tracking are answerable without issuing a command: whose turn and what phase; every player's money/VP/goods/assistants (by zone)/buildings/President location; the tracks (church/customs, with the 2P dummy); the port and lab displays with their actual contents; achievements and their progress. Design direction: a persistent multi-panel dashboard rather than a board-plus-one-panel layout — exact composition iterated visually (§4).

**R3 — Order tracking as a first-class surface.** A continuously visible (or one-keystroke) surface showing, per player: unfulfilled orders in hand (with their goods requirements and reward), fulfilled order count/VP, and the port displays' six cards with zone pricing — so "who needs what, and what's available" never requires reconstructing from memory or `log`.

**R4 — Input without memorized grammar.** Every decision reachable by numbered menu from the prompt; the existing direct grammars (`a`/`b`/`m`/`r`, `reveal`) remain as expert aliases but are never the only path. Prompts name their choices in words. Discoverability beats brevity everywhere.

**R5 — Use the window (owner-ruled shape, §3.1).** The current frame targets ≤140 columns and ~32–40 rows by design — a sacrifice the owner rules is made *"for no great reason."* The redesign takes a **considerably raised fixed floor as the base layout** — the bulk of the design lives at the floor, and the floor is what pays for full text and better iconography — with **adaptive improvements and visual optimizations beyond it** on larger windows. The floor's exact dimensions are a spec §0 constant, chosen at mock time from what full-text tiles plus the persistent dashboard actually need (not picked in advance); below the floor the plain rolling-text mode remains the graceful fallback.

**R6 — Streamlined two-real-player recording.** The owner's record-and-replay direction (proposal §6): live/recording entry pared to minimum ceremony so transcribing two real players is fast — reveal prompts, confirmations, and echoes redesigned for speed and confidence at the table. (The read-only replay *viewer* stays 3.5b scope; the *recording* half is this phase.)

**R7 — The honesty net holds, unchanged.** Engine as sole legality authority; piped/redirected output stays the plain, complete, escape-free line protocol (`Repl::step(&str)` remains the tested substrate); `NO_COLOR` honored; the guide pair (`docs/cli-guide.md` + `.html`) rewritten in the same commits and `guide_sync` kept green; reveal/reconciliation and desync-lockdown *correctness* untouched (they worked — the presentation around them is what changes); no new sim-record event kinds. A large re-pointing of CLI tests and the guide is **planned work, not collateral**.

## 3. Owner rulings (2026-08-05, at the brief's review — these land in the 3.5a spec §0)

1. **Layout: a considerably RAISED FIXED FLOOR as the base, adaptive beyond it.** The owner's words in substance: adaptive is ideal and achievable, but the bulk of the gain is raising the floor considerably — the current small terminal is a sacrifice *"for no great reason"* — so the main layout is designed at a larger fixed base (full text, better iconography), with adaptive improvements and visual optimizations layered above the floor for still-larger windows.
2. **Input: menus + full words.** Line-based input with every decision reachable by numbered menu and plain-word prompts; current shortcuts become optional aliases. (Raw-mode navigation stays a 3.5b-or-later candidate per the v1-beta note's deferral.)
3. **Information posture: persistent dashboard.** Board plus always-visible panels — players, orders (hands + ports + fulfillment), tracks, achievements.
4. **3.5b WAITS for the 3.5a close.** Sequential, in the owner's words: the live gate game is *"the final merge of the PR for 3.5a and that's a prereq for 3.5b and beyond."*

## 4. Process — Forge in the loop

The redesign iterates **visually**: candidate frames are driven in a real PTY via the Forge MCP (recipe: `docs/forge-cli-testing.md`), screenshotted, and compared — the owner can watch renders live at the dashboard (`http://127.0.0.1:3141/`) and rule on mocks before implementation hardens. Cadence per the 3.5 governance (proposal §3): per-task fresh implementer + independent reviewer, findings and resolutions as PR comments, six-lens at the PR; owner pause points at the design rulings above and at the mock sign-off.

## 5. Phase structure and the gate

- **Branch:** `phase3.5a-cli-redesign`; this brief + the scoping proposal are its opening docs commit.
- **PR:** opened when the redesigned CLI is playable; **merged only after the live gate game — one full physical game, start to finish, tracked on the redesigned surface without fighting the tool** — succeeds. The gate event is the PR's completion criterion; the gate-claim discipline holds (only the played game claims it).
- **At the close:** the disposition (🔶 OD-9(c) — rules/observability findings routed per the exposure map, each owner-gated) and the **3.5b handover** — a dated doc carrying: the gameplay findings and their coverage honestly stated (essentials, not every card); the changes they demand; the strength-lane plan (scoping proposal §§4–9) revised accordingly; and the standing owner-decision index.

## 6. Out of 3.5a (unchanged fences)

Bot-seat productionization, the `why` command, and the replay viewer (→ 3.5b, per the proposal); live-mode bots and live-mode `suggest` (structural fences); bot-vs-bot spectating; egui/GUI (post-bar); anything touching `SEMANTICS_REV`, the engine, or the oracle (a rules finding from the game routes through the owner-gated exposure path, never through this phase's own scope).

## 7. Success criteria

The gate text, plus the owner-readable form of R1–R5: the whole game state readable at a glance; any table move enterable without memorized grammar; orders trackable continuously; the frame filling the owner's actual window. The owner's judgment at the table is the criterion — the same principle as the acceptance play's rubric.

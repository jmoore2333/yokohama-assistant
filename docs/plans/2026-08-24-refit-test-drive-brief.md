## 1. What happened

Nine turns. Red (the refit) visited the Employment Agency on turns 1, 5 and 9 — **every turn on
which it was legal** — and took the maximum assistants each time. It ended with **6 VP to 17**,
**zero goods**, **zero yen**, **zero orders filled**, **zero tech**, and more assistants in hand (7)
than the opponent (5).

The two-turn orbit is not indecision: a President may not end its turn where it started, so A05 →
elsewhere → A05 is the tightest legal cycle around a fixed preference.

## 2. Why the preference never breaks

`assistants_in_warehouse` is scored as `(mine − theirs) × w`. Draining the warehouse widens the
differential, so **each drain raises the value of the next one**, and the marginal value per
assistant is flat with no saturation (measured: +9/assistant at turn 5, +5 at turn 9, straight off
the runner-up margins). It is a farmable gradient attached to a free, repeatable action.

**The warehouse weight is not the bug.** Refit early `-9`; shipped V2 early `-10`. They agree.
What differs is everything the pump has to outbid:

| early weight | V2 | refit |
|---|---|---|
| country_bonus_now | **20** | 3 |
| tech_bonus_now | **14** | 1 |
| shophouses_built | **12** | 0 |
| five_power_tokens | **8** | −3 |
| assistants_in_warehouse | −10 | −9 |

V2 outbids its own warehouse pump with four large competing goals that drag the president around
the board doing economy. The refit flattened all four. **No individual refit weight is indefensible;
the failure is entirely in the ratios.**

## 3. Why the fit produced those ratios (the dive)

**a. The objective measures between-game variation; play is decided by within-move variation.**
Mean per-stage AUC of ordering eventual winners above losers pins the ordering of *states that
occurred*, across *different games*. Move selection compares *sibling successors of one decision*,
which differ only in the narrow directions a single move can move. AUC leaves the score function
nearly unconstrained in exactly those directions. A vector can rank the corpus at 0.80 and still
have an arbitrary gradient where the chooser actually reads it.

**b. The corpus was generated by V2, so V2's competencies look uninformative.** In 2000 games of
V2 self-play everyone develops country sets and tech, so mid-game country/tech bonus *discriminates*
winners weakly — low variance, low AUC contribution — and gets flattened. That is the fit correctly
reporting "this doesn't separate winners **among players who all already do it**", and the chooser
then reading it as "this doesn't matter." Textbook off-policy distribution shift: the fitted table
immediately walks off the distribution it was fit on, and it is 11 VP behind by turn 9.

**c. Degenerate columns absorb arbitrary coefficients.** `assistants_in_hand_core` = `min(in_hand, 2)`
sits pinned at 2 for both seats, so its differential is 0 and its product is 0 whatever the weight.
The fit put **−63** there — the largest magnitude in the vector — because a near-constant column
costs the objective nothing. `eval.rs:215` already says this about the feature ("very nearly the
CONSTANT 2, and a constant separates nothing whatever weight it carries"), and the v2 freeze
measured it no-signal (late AUC 0.4877). **The refit's headline number is its least constrained
one, and it never fires in play.** Treat it as a diagnostic that the fit is under-regularized, not
as a discovery.

**d. The basis has no term for the economy.** None of the sixteen incumbent features scores goods,
money, or whether a held order can actually be filled. V2 covered the hole *by proxy* through
country/tech bonus. Flatten the proxies and the hole is exposed: this bot has no representation of
the fact that it is broke. In the final reads `money_stock` 0-vs-3, `trade_goods_stock` 0-vs-3 and
`order_matched_goods` 0-vs-1 all sat there weighted **zero**.

**e. Unreachable weights are inert.** `trading_houses_built` +32 (V2: 15) never fired because the
feature counts BUILT houses and the route runs through a token that scores nothing — at turn 5 the
bot explicitly priced "take 2 assistants + 1 shophouse, cost 2 yen" at −13 and declined. A weight on
a state you cannot reach in one turn's lookahead is not a strategy.

## 5. Leads, cheapest first

1. **Get the number.** `examples/arena cell` with `v2-refit-padded` vs `v2-padded`, swapped-balanced,
   `--os-entropy` master recorded before use. One cell settles "is the refit a worse player" in a way
   one game cannot. Expectation after the above: badly worse at 2P.
2. **Audit the fit matrix for degenerate columns.** Per-feature variance and leverage over the
   step-6 matrix. Any near-constant column is a free parameter; `assistants_in_hand_core` is one and
   there may be others. Then either drop them, or regularize (ridge/L1) so magnitude has to be earned.
3. **Change the objective to match the use.** The table is used to rank *siblings of one decision*.
   Fit that: pairwise ranking over successors of the same decision point labelled by eventual result
   (Bradley-Terry / learning-to-rank), not AUC over pooled states. This is the single change most
   likely to make a fitted table playable.
4. **Iterate the corpus.** Refit on games generated by the fitted table and repeat. Distribution
   shift is the named disease; self-play iteration is the standard cure.
5. **Add a pump detector to the protocol.** For each feature, ask: is there a repeatable, low-cost,
   legal action that monotonically improves its differential without bound? If yes, it needs a cap,
   a saturating transform, or a competing cost. `assistants_in_warehouse` is the worked example.
6. **This is a motivating case for V4's sighted groups.** `money_stock`, `trade_goods_stock`,
   `order_matched_goods`, `executable_orders` are precisely the terms whose absence let this happen.
   Worth citing when the economic/order-opportunity group is argued.

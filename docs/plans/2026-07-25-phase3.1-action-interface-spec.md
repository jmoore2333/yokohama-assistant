## 2. Verified fact base (measured; policy stated per figure)

**F1 — The worst case is confirmed and is TWO numbers, not one.** The retreat family `ReturnPresidentToHand { recover: BTreeMap<AreaId,u8> }` (`types/moves.rs:84-86`) is generated by `enumerate_recovery_options` (`legal_moves.rs:382-400`) as the **exact Cartesian product** `∏(count_a + 1)` over occupied areas — proven exact for four distributions. Worst case = 20 assistants as three count-2 + fourteen count-1 over the 17 placeable 4P areas = `2¹⁴·3³ = 442,368` retreat moves. **But the whole-call ceiling splits by president position** (both measured on the *same* saturated state, worst-case construction, this machine, release):
- **President InHand:** 442,385 moves / **~21 ms** / **~1.02 M allocations** (retreat product only; +17 `PlacePresident`).
- **President OnBoard:** 442,430 moves / **~65 ms** / **~1.57 M allocations** — the extra ~44 ms / ~0.55 M allocs is `enumerate_president_routes` (`movement.rs:453`), an all-simple-paths DFS that explodes with own-assistant density even though its *output* stays ≤16 labels (§F3). The map's collapsed "39 ms / 1.16 M" is an unstated average of the two. **3.1's recorded latency/allocation bound must use the OnBoard figure**, and the **route family needs a lazy enumerator too** — retreat alone only halves the worst case.

**F2 — Retreat is a clean product space, servable without materialization.** A candidate is a chosen count `k_a ∈ [0, count_a]` per occupied area, independent across areas. A direct `BTreeMap` constructor + mixed-radix index (area `a` = digit in radix `count_a+1`, iterating `on_board` BTreeMap order to match enumeration order) + independent per-area sampling all serve one candidate in **~333 ns** vs **~18.7 ms** to materialize the InHand family — a ~56,000× gap. The complete legality condition is the existing self-defending raw arm `apply_return_to_hand` (`apply_move.rs:634-639`: per-area `count ∈ [1, have]`); no new validator needed for retreat.

**F3 — Routes: tiny output, eager-DFS latency.** `enumerate_president_routes` yields ≤16 (organic 21; adversarial-constructed 519 — the `routes3_worstcase.rs` battery bound) distinct `(destination, fee-vector)` labels, flat across president money 0→50 (no fee cliff). But its time is `O(all simple paths)` on the board graph: measured 500 ns → 20 ms as own-assistant density rises from 3 to 17 areas, un-pruned by money on a dense board (mostly-free edges). The lazy fix: enumerate reachable destinations cheaply (`find_reachable_areas_with_tech`, `movement.rs:128`, polynomial) then attach the (rare) per-destination fee-vector variants — preserving the L2 income-denial labels without the exponential path walk.

**F4 — Placement is combinations; its real ceiling is ~4× the quoted mean.** `PlaceThreeAreas` (Option A) = all k-subsets of ~17 placeable areas, `k ∈ 0..=min(3, in_hand)` (4 with Newspaper), affordability-gated; `PlaceTwoSameArea` (Option B) = per-area + Telegram neighbors. The map/CLAUDE.md "~191–829" is a **no-Newspaper uniform per-player MEAN**; the measured uniform **max is 3231** (4P + Newspaper 4-area = C(17,1..4) + Option B + empty). Maps cleanly onto the combinatorial number system (colex index = the `j<k<l` loop order). Constructor validated by the complete `apply_placement` arm (`apply_move.rs:428-445` + distinctness/cap `:51-61` + `validate_telegram_bonus` `:320-347`).

**F5 — Open-decision #3 is asymmetric: `apply_move_unchecked` is NOT a complete legality oracle.** Measured over 861,188 uniform-self-play states: constructing an *illegal* candidate and calling `apply_move_unchecked` FALSE-ACCEPTS it (applies it though `get_legal_moves` excludes it) in **four dimensions** — Building `power≥4` (457), on-board movement assistant-chain reachability (22,536), `PlacePresident` possible-action-area (171,984), and **phase** (2,274; no raw arm checks `turn_phase`). This is the documented "Tier C" residue (`apply_move.rs:362-364`): Phase-2.9 hardened only conservation/structural dimensions (placement distinctness, retreat bounds, area-action power — all **0** false-accepts, verified). So:
- **"Design B as a universal oracle" (validate any candidate by applying it) is UNSOUND.** Do not adopt.
- **"Design B as construction-correct" (the map's literal §5 words)** is sound *iff the constructor itself guarantees phase + the three Tier-C dimensions*, leaving `apply_move_unchecked` to validate the affordability/conservation residue (which it does completely). Cost: a full `GameState` clone per candidate (~380 ns, flat in list size).
- **"Design A" (`is_move_legal(&GameState, &Move, &Rules) -> bool`, allocation-free predicate)** re-checks phase + family + all semantic dims without cloning, reusing the existing pure-predicate surface (`can_fulfill_order`, `is_possible_action_area_with_tech`, `validate_port/lab/church/customs_action`, `calculate_placement_cost`, `can_teleport_*`). It requires **exactly one genuinely-new predicate**: single-candidate on-board movement reachability (today only the *enumerators* know reachability — there is no `is_reachable(state, path, dest)`).

**F6 — Enumeration order is deterministic and BTreeMap-canonical, with one exception.** Every generator feeds off BTreeMaps / fixed arrays / ascending ranges EXCEPT the `FulfillOrder` loop (`legal_moves.rs:59`) over `order_cards_in_hand: Vec<OrderCardId>` (insertion order, history-dependent). The factored↔exhaustive equivalence test must replicate the hand Vec order for `FulfillOrder`, not a sorted key. (The Chinatown `HashSet` at `:836` is a membership guard, never iterated for emission — not an order carrier.)

**F7 — Distributions for widening schedules (measured, uniform-self-play policy, stated).** Placement family mean 515.7 / max 3231; Movement family mean 58.52 / max 2314, of which the retreat family alone is ~92–99% (mean 54.07 / max 2304). Under a *non-volunteering-retreat* probe (guidingpath:173) 4P option counts reach median 17,287 / max 147,467. **Both are policy-conditional**; the widening schedules 3.3+ derive from them must state their policy. 3.1 records the distributions and the constructors that make widening *possible* (indexable families); it does not implement a widening policy (that is search, 3.3).

**F8 — Constraints (verified).** (a) checked `apply_move` (`apply_move.rs:222-227`) re-materializes the full list every call (~12 µs @851 moves; ~28.8 ms at the retreat worst state) — the search inner loop must never call it. (b) The exhaustive `get_legal_moves -> Vec<Move>` surface has 234 consumer references (CLI render/repl/reveal, core tests, difftest, oracle differential) — frozen, the factored source is purely additive. (c) `SEMANTICS_REV` stays 3: a factored enumerator yielding the same move SET changes no `apply_move` result, no replay. (d) Oracle exposure is zero (the oracle re-derives its own legality, imports types-only, allowlist-enforced) — the factored module must NOT enter the oracle's import set. (e) `GameState` clone 0.36/0.58/0.67 µs at 2/3/4P (~⅔ is `board_layout`) — the per-candidate clone floor design B pays.

**F9 — Benchmark surface is absent.** No `benches/`, no criterion, no `#[bench]`, no `black_box` anywhere. The only perf code is `examples/selfplay.rs` (`Instant` + moves/sec). Idiomatic 3.1 additions (zero new deps — criterion would break the house style): a release `examples/action_cost.rs` (Instant + a ~40-line counting global allocator for the allocation half) and a two-tier `#[ignore]` release-gate test modeled on `routes3_worstcase.rs` (a per-edit smoke bound + a release-tier maximal bound, both constructing an adversarial state from public constructors and asserting a recorded bound).

## 3. Design

### 3.1 Architecture

A new engine-side module `game/search_actions.rs` (flat `mod`/`pub use` per convention; names `ActionSource`, `MoveFamily` are free — `Policy` is difftest-owned and reserved for 3.2, `actions`/`MoveSource` are taken). It operates over `&GameState` + `&Rules` (a sampled world from 3.0's `BeliefSampler`; legality is view-measurable, F5-of-3.0) and is **purely additive** — `get_legal_moves` is untouched and remains the authority `apply_move` checks against.

The core insight from F5: build **construction-correct** family sources (they emit only legal candidates by construction, including the Tier-C dimensions), so a chosen candidate needs only the cheap affordability/conservation residue validated. Then the A-vs-B question (§3.6) is narrowed to how the *search* re-validates a candidate it constructed, and settled by microbenchmark.

### 3.2 Families first — `MoveFamily` (3.1a)

`fn families(&GameState, &Rules) -> Vec<MoveFamily>` mirrors `get_legal_moves`' queue-first + phase dispatch (`legal_moves.rs:9-24`) but yields **family tags**, not moves. (`Vec`, not `SmallVec` — smallvec is not a dependency and the house style is dependency-minimal; `families()` is called once per decision point, never in the hot loop, so one small Vec is negligible.) `enum MoveFamily` tags each decision-phase family (additional-action, placement, movement-routes, movement-retreat, area-action, five-power, building, and the pending-choice-resolution families). A family tag carries an **owned** snapshot of what it needs (e.g. the retreat family owns a `BTreeMap<AreaId,u8>` clone of the ≤17-entry `on_board` — an owned clone sidesteps any borrow/lifetime issue in the returned iterators). **The queue short-circuit is mirrored:** when a `PendingChoice` heads the queue, `families()` yields only the choice-resolution family (matching `legal_moves.rs:9-11`), so per-family equivalence works for queue-head families too.

Per family: `fn family_len(&GameState, &Rules, &MoveFamily) -> usize` (exact count where the family is a clean space — the retreat product `∏(count_a+1)` — or a documented **upper bound** where affordability punches holes, placement `Σ C(n,k)` before per-combination fee gating, see §3.4); `fn iter_family(state, rules, family) -> impl Iterator<Item = Move>` (lazy paging, one legal move at a time, no backing Vec — applies any per-combination filter inline); `fn sample_family(state, rules, family, rng) -> Move` (O(family arity) draw; rejection-free only for clean spaces, generate-and-filter for affordability-gated ones — §3.4).

### 3.3 Retreat — direct constructor + mixed-radix index (the six-figure family, 3.1a)

`fn retreat_move(on_board: &BTreeMap<AreaId,u8>, per_area_counts: &[(AreaId,u8)]) -> Move` builds the one `ReturnPresidentToHand` without enumeration. `iter_family`/`sample_family` for retreat use mixed-radix decoding over `on_board` BTreeMap order (the **last** BTreeMap area is the least-significant digit; digit `k_a` in radix `count_a+1`) so paging index `i` in `[0, ∏(count_a+1))` reproduces `enumerate_recovery_options` order exactly (`legal_moves.rs:382-400`). Sampling draws each `k_a` independently — **no rejection, every draw legal** (the only complete-product-space family). Validated by the existing complete raw arm `apply_return_to_hand` (`apply_move.rs:634-639`). **Never materialized as a Vec.** The family the gate's zero-heap-growth bound targets, and the one that discharges the mandatory six-figure handover.

### 3.4 Placement — combination construction with per-combination affordability (3.1a)

`fn place_combo(areas_sorted: &[AreaId], selection: &[usize]) -> Move` and combination iteration over the placeable set, reproducing the `k=0..K` colex loop order (`legal_moves.rs:147-195`). **Affordability is a per-COMBINATION gate, not a per-area filter:** `calculate_placement_cost` (`legal_moves.rs:99`) sums 1 Yen per (placed-area, opponent-president) pair over *all* placed areas, so a k-subset of individually-affordable areas can still exceed money. The design honors this: (a) placement `family_len` is an **upper bound** `Σ C(n,k)` — affordable subsets aren't contiguous in colex order; (b) `iter_family` is a **lazy generate-and-filter** — iterate colex combinations, drop each failing `calculate_placement_cost`, staying lazy exactly as `legal_moves.rs` does; (c) placement `sample_family` is **NOT rejection-free** (a random affordable-per-area k-subset can be over-budget) — only §3.3 retreat is. Option B (`PlaceTwoSameArea` + Telegram non-crossing neighbors) enumerates directly (small). 3.1a provides the indexable structure + the honest per-combination filter; any rank/unrank that skips unaffordable combos for O(1) paging is a 3.3-widening concern.

### 3.5 Routes — lazy label enumerator (3.1b — the second explosion, and the research-risk piece)

**Genuinely unsolved-in-spec; deferred to 3.1b; flagged as a possible re-map signal.** The naive approach ("enumerate destinations via `find_reachable_areas_with_tech` then attach fee-vector variants") does **not** work: `find_reachable_areas_with_tech` (`movement.rs:128`, doc `:187-190`) is a min-cost Dijkstra returning exactly **one** path/fee-vector per destination — it discards precisely the L2 income-denial variants that `enumerate_president_routes` (`movement.rs:453`) exists to capture (its doc `:425-432`: "a deliberately pricier route that pays a DIFFERENT fee recipient — or simply pays more — is real, enumerable play … every affordable label must survive, not just the cheapest"). The route *label* is `(destination, RouteVector)`, a whole-path fee profile.

So 3.1b must design a **correct cheap enumerator of the distinct affordable labels** (find_reachable supplies only the destination *set*). Tractable structure to exploit: label components are small and bounded — bank toll = a small int (canal crossings), each opponent president sits on exactly one area so its fee is 0/1 on any simple path — so the distinct achievable `RouteVector`s per destination are far fewer (≤~519 constructed / ≤21 organic) than the simple paths, suggesting a bounded search over `(area, toll-profile)` product-nodes or an affordability-pruned memoized walk rather than the naive 17!-path DFS. **3.1b's spec must present a real algorithm and prove it reproduces `enumerate_president_routes`' exact label set; if a correct cheap enumerator proves infeasible, that is a re-map signal (stop — do NOT silently fall back to the gate-forbidden DFS or a quiet cap).** The exhaustive `enumerate_president_routes` stays as the frozen `get_legal_moves` surface + equivalence oracle. Teleports (Station/Tram, `path: []`) enumerate directly.

### 3.6 Candidate validation — open-decision #3 (3.1b), settled by microbenchmark

Two halves, benchmark-settled:

1. **Construction-correct sources** (§3.3–3.5 + small families enumerated directly) — emit only legal candidates, so `apply_move_unchecked` validates only the affordability/conservation residue (which it does completely). The map's literal "design B"; always available for the construction-correct families.
2. **`is_move_legal(&GameState, &Move, &Rules) -> bool`** (design A) — an allocation-free predicate reusing the existing pure-predicate surface (F5). Its one genuinely-new piece, **`is_president_path_legal`**, has a contract the review corrected — "can_step_onto per hop + affordability" is **incomplete** and would false-accept. The complete single-candidate on-board-movement check is: (i) consecutive-hop adjacency + simple-path + no-return-to-start (`validate_movement_destination`, `apply_move.rs:243-250`); (ii) `can_step_onto` per intermediate hop (`movement.rs:209-211`); (iii) **destination is a Possible Action Area** — `is_possible_action_area_full` (`movement.rs:63-71`: own assistant AND no opponent president unless Postal), WITHOUT which it false-accepts a path ending on an own-assistant area an opponent president blocks (the F5 movement/PlacePresident false-accept class); (iv) affordability. Plus **canonicalization:** `get_legal_moves` emits one lex-least path per `(destination, RouteVector)` label, so the predicate/equivalence test compares by label or canonicalizes the path, never by raw path.

The **microbenchmark** (§4) measures, per family, design A's no-clone predicate against design B's ~380 ns clone-per-candidate floor, at ordinary and worst-case states; the **decision is recorded from the measured numbers** (decision-log row at 3.1b execution), not pre-judged. Research prior (to confirm/overturn): A wins the inner loop (B clones even to reject and needs the same Tier-C-correct construction anyway). The exhaustive checked `apply_move` boundary stays the ultimate authority, untouched.

## 4. Gate (map §5 3.1, made executable — met across 3.1a + 3.1b)

- **Worst-case latency/allocation bounds recorded and met.** A release-tier `#[ignore]` test (routes3_worstcase.rs pattern) + an `examples/action_cost.rs` curve: construct the 4P **OnBoard** saturated worst case; assert the factored source serves a retreat decision (3.1a) and a route decision (3.1b) with **bounded, near-constant allocations** (no ∏-materialization, no simple-path DFS) — e.g. a retreat candidate in O(#areas) allocations regardless of on-board spread — while recording the exhaustive `get_legal_moves` ceiling (~442k moves / ~65 ms / ~1.5 M allocs) it avoids. **Meter pinned:** the byte meter is **peak outstanding requested bytes** (a running-outstanding counter + fetch-max in the counting `GlobalAlloc`; ~55 MB in research) — explicitly NOT RSS (unmeasurable dep-free; the map's "68.3 MB peak" is plausibly RSS and is not the target). The **primary hard assertion is the allocation COUNT bound** (deterministic across runs); bytes and wall-clock are recorded as curves, not hard gates (machine-variable). A per-edit smoke runs a smaller bound.
- **Factored ↔ exhaustive equivalence, per family, as multisets.** For each `MoveFamily`: `{factored family, fully enumerated via iter_family} == {get_legal_moves(state) filtered to that family}` as multisets, reusing the difftest `compare.rs` key/normalize/set-diff pattern (`move_key`/`diff_key_multisets`). Order/index round-trips: `iter_family` order reproduces `get_legal_moves` order per family (BTreeMap-canonical); mixed-radix / combination index ↔ enumerated position agree. Run across seeded states at 2/3/4P including constructed adversarial retreat/route/placement states. **Anti-vacuity, pinned explicitly:** (a) assert the exploding families (retreat ≥ tens-of-thousands, route ≥ its adversarial label count, placement ≥ C(17,3)) were exercised at scale — a multiset test over empty families is vacuous; (b) the **FulfillOrder order round-trip runs on a fixture with ≥2 orders in NON-sorted hand order** (F6) — the multiset test is order-insensitive, so a factored iterator that emitted orders sorted-by-id would pass a naive round-trip on a sorted-hand fixture silently.
- **Zero semantic change.** `SEMANTICS_REV` untouched (3); the differential release gates stay green (the oracle compares the *unchanged* `get_legal_moves`); no engine legality/apply/scoring/setup edit — the only apply-path additions are pure predicates (`is_move_legal` / `is_president_path_legal`, 3.1b) that mutate nothing.
- **Repo gates:** debug suites + clippy `-D warnings` per task; all release-tier gates green before each PR merges. Whether the action-cost worst-case test becomes a **standing release gate** (documented at the four gate-doc sites like 3.0's 8th) or an **example-only measurement** is decided in the 3.1a plan (leaning: a standing per-edit smoke + release bound, since it is the executable proof the handover is discharged).

## 6. Oracle impact & SEMANTICS_REV assessment (map §4)

- **Oracle impact: none.** The factored source is engine-side search infrastructure; the oracle re-derives its own legality and never imports it. The `oracle_independence.rs` allowlist is untouched; differential gates re-run at close as the zero-semantic-change evidence.
- **SEMANTICS_REV: no bump.** Litmus (`semantics.rs:33-37`): nothing alters what any record replays to. The new `is_move_legal`/`is_president_path_legal` predicates are read-only and mutate no state. **Do NOT harden a Tier-C raw arm in 3.1** to close the F5 false-accepts — but the reason is NOT the semantics-rev litmus (records carry only legal moves, and the added check is a no-op for a legal move, so no replay changes and it would technically not bump): the real reasons are (1) it changes `apply_move_unchecked`'s documented Tier-C contract (`apply_move.rs:362-364`), and (2) it moves reachability/power/phase cost onto the blessed fast path that the fuzzer, self-play, and every search rollout use — a real perf regression on the hot path 3.1 exists to make fast. Closing a Tier-C false-accept is therefore out of 3.1's scope (a re-map signal), for contract + perf reasons, not replay-semantics reasons.

## 7. Known facts carried to the ledger

- **L1 — Two-number worst case.** The gate bounds the OnBoard case (~65 ms / ~1.5 M allocs); the route family's lazy enumerator is as load-bearing as the retreat constructor (F1/F3). Both must be delivered.
- **L2 — Tier-C false-accepts are real and documented.** `apply_move_unchecked` is not a legality oracle (F5); the spec relies on construction-correctness + the checked boundary, NOT on hardening the raw arms (that would be a semantics change). Recorded so no implementer "fixes" a Tier-C arm.
- **L3 — Placement real ceiling 3231, not ~829** (F4); widening schedules (3.3) derive from C(k,4) growth and the measured distributions (F7), with policy stated.
- **L4 — Allocation meter pinned** to peak-outstanding-requested-bytes + primary assertion on allocation COUNT (§4); not RSS.
- **L5 — The clone/`board_layout` Arc-split perf card** (map §8 #6) is adjacent (design B's clone floor is ~⅔ board_layout); noted, not played in 3.1 (it touches save-format compat — a deliberate later decision).
- **L6 — Map citation bug** (§ header note): map §5 3.1 cites `guidingpath.md:171(b)` (the 3.2 family-weighting item) where it means `172(b)` (the action-interface item); fixed in 3.1a's doc-sync.
- **L7 — Route enumerator may be a re-map signal** (§3.5): 3.1b must present a correct cheap label enumerator or stop and re-map; no silent DFS fallback or cap.

# Phase 2.9 — Rules ruling list for Jeremy

**What this is:** genuine rules ambiguities surfaced by the differential-oracle campaign
(plan `2026-07-20-phase-2.9-differential-oracle-plan.md`) that neither the rulebook, the KB,
nor the decision log settles. Per the phase's four-way triage, these are **collected, not
guessed** — each entry gives the exact text, both readings, what each implementation currently
does, and what turns on the answer. Rule on them whenever convenient; each answer flips at most
one condition in code. **A1 is the only one that diverged in live sweeps**; A2/A3 were caught
at spec-authoring time and both implementations currently agree on a documented reading.

Living document: later stages append. Status legend: 🔴 live divergence (interim in place) ·
🟡 agreed-but-unadjudicated reading · 🟢 RULED by Jeremy (2026-07-20, live session).

**2026-07-20 RULINGS (Jeremy, as CORRECTED same session):** Jeremy's first message ruled A1
Reading 1; on the scope question he **retracted and reversed it**: "the overarching rule for an
assistant must be present is NOT overridden by the station card — the president must only travel
to VALID locations… the station card adds one power, but like a trading house or shop, that only
adds power, not the assistant requirement," quoting the rulebook's Movement text as the
governing authority (legal destination = ≥1 own assistant + no opposing president; each transited
area needs ≥1 own assistant; 1 Yen per opposing president transited, pay or may not pass; no
return to start; no reachable legal destination ⇒ the return-to-hand option is mandatory).

Final rulings: **A1(i) → Reading 2** (own assistant REQUIRED at the Station area; the teleport's
card text still governs what it explicitly says — free, and no payment for other President pawns
there). **A1(ii) → no special from-hand move**: with the President in hand, assistants place
first, then the President places to a valid location per the normal rules. **A2 → count ALL
owned mercenaries** (face-up or face-down) toward Tokens5. **A3 → sequential and independent**
Chinatown resolution. Station token placed on acquisition and immovable (confirms StationPlace).

Implementation impact: **ZERO behavior changes** — the engine implemented Reading 2 and normal
from-hand rules all along, and the oracle's PENDING-RULING interim mirrored exactly the ruled
behavior; A2/A3 confirm both sides. The markers/tests convert from "pending" to "ruled" pins,
and the decision log gains the ruling row. (The earlier commit recording Reading 1 is superseded

## A1 🟢 RULED — Station teleport preconditions (spec §7.1-A1; sweep signature 2, 3/60 games)

> **Ruling (Jeremy, 2026-07-20, CORRECTED same session):** the first message ruled Reading 1;
> on the scope question Jeremy retracted the "no assistants needed" phrasing as wrong and ruled
> **Reading 2**: the general assistant-at-destination rule is NOT overridden by the Station card
> — like a shophouse or trading house, the station token adds POWER only. The card's explicit
> clauses stand (free; no payment for other President pawns there). Question (ii): **no special
> from-hand move** — assistants place first, the President then places to a valid location per
> the normal rules. Token placed on acquisition, immovable. He quoted the rulebook Movement text
> verbatim as the governing authority (legal destination = ≥1 own assistant + no opposing
> president; transit needs ≥1 own assistant per area; 1¥ per opposing president transited or may
> not pass; no return to start; no reachable destination ⇒ return-to-hand is mandatory).
> **Net code impact: zero** — the engine's inception-era Reading 2 is confirmed correct, and the
> oracle's triage interim mirrored exactly this; markers convert from PENDING-RULING to RULED.
> A worked example in the ruling also re-confirmed two adjudicated rules: assistant placement
> into an opposing-president area costs 1¥ to that player (C1), and taking a turn in an area
> with another player's Trading House costs the actor nothing (passive income is paid to the
> owner from the supply — engine verified at apply_move.rs apply_trading_house_passive_income).

**Exact rules text.** RB p.15, Station (complete): "When implementing this Technology, place
the Station token in an Area of your choice. This Station token counts as 1 additional Power
for all players in this Area. You may move your President pawn to this area during the
Movement Step for free, and you do not need to pay even if there are other President pawns in
the Area. However, you may only move to this Area if you are doing this Area action." The
general rule it may or may not override — RB p.6, Movement: "At least one of that player's
Assistant pawns must be in the destination area." Comparison card — RB p.16 Tram, equally
silent on assistants, was ruled assistant-free (decision log 2026-07-17 I1).

**Question (i): does the Station teleport require ≥1 of YOUR assistants at the Station area?**
- *Reading 1 — no:* the card text is a complete condition list; its only stated restriction is
  must-do-this-area's-action (structurally guaranteed: Movement is followed by the mandatory
  AreaAction, and President + Station token = Power 2, so the action is always performable).
  Tram analog. The oracle implemented this; the spec flagged it deliberately so it would
  surface.
- *Reading 2 — yes:* the card explicitly overrides only the fee and other-presidents clauses
  of p.6's stop rule; the assistant clause is never mentioned, so it silently persists. The
  ENGINE implements this (`yokohama-core/src/game/movement.rs:245-250`); its inception test
  presupposed the assistant as setup — the reading was assumed, never adjudicated.

**Question (ii): may the teleport be taken with the President in hand?** Both implementations
currently say no (board-only). No source states it; noted so it doesn't resurface in Phase 3.
The from-hand-Tram "changes nothing" rationale does NOT transfer — from hand, Station WOULD
change something (free entry to a non-Possible-Action Area).

**Live distinguishing states (sweep artifacts, all byte-identical on repro):**
`crates/yokohama-difftest/fixtures/ruling-a1/6-4-retreat-averse-269.json` (turn 46: P1 owns Station,
token at area 1, zero P1 assistants there, no opponent president there — Reading 1 offers the
free move, Reading 2 does not); `7-4-retreat-averse-130.json` (turn 23, same shape);
`7-4-uniform-30.json` (turn 7, station area 8, same shape). 3 of 60 sweep games hit it
organically — this is not a constructed corner.

**Play implications.** The readings differ exactly when the Station area holds none of the
owner's assistants — common after Recovery sweeps them home. Reading 1 makes the Station a
standing re-entry point (free Power-2 landing, free entry past an opponent president's stop
fee) after the owner vacates; Reading 2 makes the tech markedly weaker late-game. A bot
trained under Reading 2 never learns the vacated-station tactic and underprices the tech; if
the table plays Reading 1, live tracking would reject a legal physical move mid-game. A
Reading-1-trained bot at a Reading-2 table attempts illegal teleports.

**Interim in place (2026-07-20 triage):** the oracle temporarily mirrors the engine's
Reading 2 behind a `PENDING-RULING §7.1-A1` marker so the differential gate binds. The engine
is deliberately untouched — an engine change would silently bake an unadjudicated reading into
`rules_hash`-stamped records. **Your ruling flips exactly one condition** on whichever side
loses (Reading 1 ⇒ delete the engine's assistant gate, failing test first; Reading 2 ⇒ delete
the oracle's PENDING-RULING marker, keeping its gate).

## A2 🟢 RULED — Tokens5 achievement vs face-down mercenaries (spec §7.1-A2)

> **Ruling (Jeremy, 2026-07-20):** face-up or face-down, all owned Foreign Mercenary tokens
> count toward the achievement. Confirms both implementations (count-all); pin tests added in
> the ruling wave.

**Exact rules text.** RB p.14: "Own a total of 5 5-Power and/or Foreign Mercenary tokens."
Used mercenaries are flipped face-down (RB p.8); "keep … in their possession" is stated only
for 5-Power tokens. End-game remainder scoring separately says "**Unused** Foreign Mercenary
tokens" (RB p.10) — the rulebook distinguishes the notions when it wants to, weak evidence
for count-all here.

**Readings.** Count all owned tokens (face-up + face-down), vs count face-up mercenaries only.
**Distinguishing state:** 3 five-power tokens + 2 used mercenaries — claimable under count-all
only.

**Current state:** BOTH the engine and the oracle count all owned tokens (agreement verified by
the sweeps — zero divergence), so nothing is blocked; the reading is simply unadjudicated. If
you rule face-up-only, both sides change together (engine failing-test-first).

## A3 🟢 RULED — Chinatown sequential proceeds (spec §7.1-A3)

> **Ruling (Jeremy, 2026-07-20):** Chinatown trades resolve sequentially and independently —
> the sequential reading is correct. Confirms both implementations.

**Exact rules text.** RB p.13: "The number of times you may exchange depends on the Action
Power … For each exchange, you may do one of the following."

**Readings.** Sequential — exchanges resolve one at a time and later exchanges may spend
earlier proceeds; vs budget — every exchange must be payable from pre-action holdings.
**Distinguishing state:** 0 Yen + 1 Copper at power 2 — "sell copper (+2¥), buy silk (−2¥)"
is legal only under the sequential reading.

**Current state (corrected at round-2 triage):** BOTH implementations confirmedly use the
SEQUENTIAL running-balance reading (engine: BFS over running simulated resources,
`legal_moves.rs generate_chinatown_actions`; oracle: DFS over running balances,
`area_actions_market.rs chinatown_moves`). Round-2 sweeps DID surface Chinatown divergences,
but they are ORTHOGONAL to the sequential/budget question and were plain bugs, not this
ambiguity: (i) the oracle omitted the Mining Technology rider on YenToCopper (class-2 oracle
bug, fixed — see below); (ii) the engine's `CHINATOWN_ENUM_DEPTH = 4` hid legal length-5
multisets at power 5 that its own validator (len ≤ power) accepts (engine completeness gap,
fixed). A3 itself remains unadjudicated-but-agreed; a budget ruling would change both sides
together.

## Not ambiguities (for the record)

- **Building slot choice (round-1 signature 1, 56/60 games) — ENGINE BUG, fixed this phase.**
  RB p.7 settles it decisively ("onto an empty Shophouse space … gain the benefits stated on
  the space that YOU built your Shophouse on"; no fill-order rule exists anywhere): every empty
  slot is a distinct legal choice with materially different printed rewards. The engine's
  first-empty-slot pinning was an unflagged residual auto-resolution — no decision-log row ever
  sanctioned it. Fixed engine-side with per-slot enumeration + raw-arm self-defense; the CLI's
  prefix-packed build marks (which pinned the old premise by design) reworked in the same wave.
- **Chinatown Mining rider (round-2 facet A, 5 games) — ORACLE BUG, fixed.** Already
  adjudicated: Mining Technology's "gain 1 additional Copper" applies to EVERY copper receipt
  (decision log 2026-07-18), including Chinatown's 2¥→Copper buy. The oracle's spec transcribed
  the Chinatown board's printed rates and missed the cross-cutting tech rider (it lives on the
  card and in the decision log, not on the board) — the misreading itself is evidence the
  interaction is easy to overlook at a table.
- **Chinatown depth cap (round-2 facet B, 12 games) — ENGINE BUG, fixed.** The enumerator's
  `CHINATOWN_ENUM_DEPTH = 4` contradicted the engine's own validator (length ≤ power) and RB
  p.13's power cap: legal, apply-valid 5-exchange plays at power 5 were hidden from the legal
  list — unrecordable in live tracking and invisible to a bot. Enumerator now runs to
  power-depth with a provably-lossless multiset-frontier prune adopted FROM the oracle's
  algorithm.

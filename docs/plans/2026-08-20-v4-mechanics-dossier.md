### Employment Agency — exactly what it moves, and what each knob is keyed to  `[CONFIRMED]`

The Employment Agency action moves tokens from the acting player's WAREHOUSE tile to their HAND — all three token classes in one basket: assistant pawns, shophouse tokens, AND trading-house tokens (`AreaAction::EmploymentAgency { assistants, shophouses, trading_houses }`). Nothing is placed on the board and nothing goes to the warehouse. TWO INDEPENDENT KNOBS, and they are the source of the orchestrator's confusion: (1) POWER keys the total NUMBER of tokens takeable in one action — 0/0/1/2/3/4 at power 0/1/2/3/4/5+, capped at 4, and the cap is on the SUM a+s+t, not per class; (2) the printed 4/5/6/7 progression is NOT keyed to power at all — it is the per-token COST of a trading house, indexed by `already_taken = in_hand + total_built` (how many of that player's 4 trading houses have already left the warehouse), summed across a multi-token take (take 2 with none taken yet = 4+5 = 9 Yen). COSTS: assistants FREE; shophouses a FLAT 2 Yen EACH (no progression — the deliberate asymmetry with trading houses); trading houses the escalating progression; total cost paid to the BANK (money is destroyed, no player is credited). `{0,0,0}` is validator-ILLEGAL — `AreaAction::Skip` is the sole canonical decline (Task-17 ruling). Building the token later from hand is FREE — the progression was already paid here.

### Placement fees — payer, payee, and the per-assistant multiplication  `[CONFIRMED]`

1 Yen per ASSISTANT PLACED to EACH opponent president occupying the target area — a genuine player-to-player transfer (payer debited, each opponent president owner credited 1 per assistant), never to the bank. Your own president never charges you. Option B (2 assistants into one area) therefore costs 2 Yen into a single-president area, not 1, and an area holding two opponent presidents costs 2 per assistant. The Telegram bonus placement (Option B's extra assistant into an adjacent area) pays the SAME fee — it routes through `apply_placement` for exactly that reason. No technology waives placement fees (Stagecoach waives MOVEMENT president fees only). Placement is from HAND only, never warehouse or management boards, and never into the Canal.

### Canal any-to-any transit, tolls, and the load-bearing adjacency fact for S4  `[CONFIRMED]`

VERIFIED AND LOAD-BEARING: `BoardLayout.adjacency` CONTAINS the canal-crossing pairs. board_gen adds each derived crossing to BOTH adjacency and canal_crossings in the same loop (board_gen.rs:335-340), and `BoardLayoutBuilder::build()` REJECTS any layout with a crossing that is not also a bidirectional adjacency edge (types/board.rs:320-326) — so 'adjacency ⊇ crossings' is an enforced invariant, not an accident. THEREFORE the free brick-offset edge set is EXACTLY `adjacency MINUS canal_crossings`, with no double-listing and no lost edge, and the correct consumer derivation is: for each `n in board_layout.adjacent_areas(a)`, keep it iff `!board_layout.is_canal_crossing(a, n)` — precisely what the Telegram generator (legal_moves.rs:240) and its apply-side twin (apply_move.rs:342) already do. The surcharge is POSITIONAL and attaches to the space, not the card: a card that slides left becomes cheaper. Required Industry Value for a purchase = the card's printed gear cost + that slot's surcharge, and nothing else.

### The purchase currency: Industry Value, and what actually pays for it  `[CONFIRMED]`

A technology is NOT bought with money directly. It is bought with INDUSTRY VALUE, assembled at the instant of purchase from exactly three sources: (1) the acting POWER at the Laboratory area, 1:1, capped at 5 (`power.min(5)`); (2) extra Yen at 2 Yen = 1 Industry Value; (3) extra Imported Goods at 1 import = 1 Industry Value. Trade goods (copper/silk/tea/seafood) buy NO Industry Value. Available IV = min(power,5) + floor(extra_yen/2) + extra_imported; the purchase is legal iff available IV >= card cost + slot surcharge. The engine additionally enforces an EXACT-COVER convention: `extra_yen` must be even (an odd Yen buys no Industry and is refused rather than silently burned), and floor(extra_yen/2) + extra_imported must not EXCEED the deficit (`deficit = required - min(power,5)`); the generator emits exactly the deficit+1 payment mixes `extra_imported = k`, `extra_yen = 2*(deficit-k)` for k in 0..=deficit, filtered by affordability.

### POWER does NOT gate which Laboratory slots are purchasable (the port-tier analogue question)  `[CONFIRMED]`

There is NO power tier at the Laboratory. Unlike the Port — where `port_accessible_zones(power)` hard-gates slots (power 0-1 = cannot take at all; 2 = Low only; 3 = Low+Mid; 4-5 = all six) — the Lab has no zone, no tier, and no minimum power. Power's ONLY role at the Lab is to contribute min(power,5) Industry Value. Every filled slot on the acting board is enumerable at every power level; a slot is unreachable only because the buyer cannot PAY the deficit in yen/imports, never because the slot is closed. Consequently a power-1 lab action can buy a 6-cost card in the 6th slot if the buyer has 14 Yen (deficit 7 → 14 yen), and a power-5 buyer with no money can still be unable to buy anything (every card in every slot costing >5). Corollary asymmetry: the Port pays a +3 VP bonus at power 5 (`execute_port_action`); the LABORATORY HAS NO 5-POWER VP BONUS at all.

### The extra-technology surcharge (D2, SEMANTICS_REV 6)  `[CONFIRMED]`

Starting with a player's 4TH technology card, every technology acquisition costs an ADDITIONAL 2 Yen OR 1 Imported Good, buyer's choice. The engine's predicate is `extra_tech_surcharge_required(tech_count) == (tech_count >= 3)` — owning 3 or more means the next card is the 4th or later. The surcharge is ON TOP of the Industry Value cover and buys NO Industry: paying it in yen adds 2 to `yen_cost`; paying it in imports requires one import BEYOND `extra_imported`. It is mandatory when due (omitting `extra_tech_payment` is refused) and REFUSED when not due (supplying it when owning <3 is an error — census gap 1: the old executor charged an unvalidated +2 and underflowed `money`). The generator forks each payment mix into the affordable surcharge variants and emits nothing for a mix where neither is affordable.

### One technology per Laboratory action; Skip; and the 2P per-turn purchase bound  `[CONFIRMED]`

A Laboratory action buys EXACTLY ONE technology card (`AreaAction::Laboratory { card_id, .. }` names one card), or `AreaAction::Skip` (always legal at the Lab, carries out the action declining everything, still sets acting area + power so later steps gate normally, and grants no area-attached bonus). There is no two-card option (that is the Port's, at 2 Yen / 1 import for the second). Cross-board buying is illegal: the executor resolves the acting Lab board (A or B) from the acting area and searches only THAT board, returning `MoveError::CardNotFound` otherwise. A second Lab action in the same turn is possible only via a mercenary sub-turn, whose target area must hold the player's own assistant and must NOT hold the player's president. Since 2P and 3P have only Lab A, this makes AT MOST ONE technology purchase per seat per turn at 2 and 3 players; only 4P (Lab A + Lab B) admits two.

### The one-technology-per-title ownership rule (C2) — exact legality site  `[CONFIRMED]`

### Does completing an order grant a mercenary — which, from where, and what if exhausted  `[CONFIRMED]`

NOT unconditionally. `check_mercenary_eligibility` sweeps ALL FIVE countries (its `_country` argument is unused — the sweep is a superset of the triggering country and is idempotent, since the check also runs on Lab acquisition, the only other icon source). For each country it counts icons across the seat's COMPLETED ORDERS plus OWNED TECHNOLOGIES, sets `threshold = (count/2)*2`, and awards one mercenary iff `threshold > (available + used) * 2` — i.e. at each EVEN count 2, 4, 6…. The mercenary is COUNTRY-MATCHED and comes from a SHARED, FINITE, PUBLIC state-level supply: English 3, American 2, French 2, German 2, Dutch 1 (10 total, contested across all seats). ### Terminal scoring — the complete final_result arithmetic  `[CONFIRMED]`

### Church and Customs majority bonuses — count, then RIGHTMOST, with the 2P dummy taking-and-consuming a place  `[CONFIRMED]`

The ranking key is (pawn COUNT descending, then RIGHTMOST occupied slot index descending) — arrival order is NOT used. ### Country set bonus — greedy 2/4/8/12 arithmetic and its marginal shape  `[CONFIRMED]`

Icons come from completed orders and owned technologies. Repeatedly: count how many of the five countries still have >= 1 icon; if fewer than 2, stop; otherwise score 12/8/4/2 for a set of 5/4/3/2 distinct countries and consume one icon of each country in that set. Icons do NOT inflate a set — four English icons are one English country, four times over across four sets. Greedy is exact here, not a heuristic: taking one of each available country is simultaneously the largest formable set and the one that leaves the most behind, so the loop is order-independent.

### Rank versus margin — margin 0 is not a verdict  `[CONFIRMED]`

Ranks come from the total ordering (total descending, then clockwise distance ascending), and distances are unique per seat, so no two seats can tie on the whole key and ranks are always unique 1..=n. The signed MARGIN (mover total minus best rival total) therefore cannot express the outcome by itself: at margin 0 the clockwise tie-break decides, and the mover may either win or lose. The harness's own margin type says so and carries `won` and `rank` beside the margin, both straight off `final_result`; 'best rival' is the highest-RANKED non-mover seat, never a bare max over totals.

And a CONFLICTING write — deleted, superseded, or a different game under the id — EVICTS the handle so a stale `Repl` cannot go on answering 200 while nothing lands (an I/O failure deliberately does NOT evict: there the handle is right and the disk is not); `create` holds its own lock across check-then-write.

(2) `web/src/*.test.tsx` renders components over jsdom using ONLY those fixtures — a payload is never hand-authored, because a builder still compiles after the server drops a field. (3) `web/e2e/` drives real Chromium against the real binary, which is the only thing that can see an image failing to load, real focus order, real layout width, and whether the SCREEN can be played to a winner. **The bot-free game is not a simplification**: `Games::create` draws its bot seed from OS entropy, so a golden built over a bot game would churn on every run — and a walk that answers every placement with "place none" never puts an assistant on the board, measured at turn 3,644 with nothing having happened.
  - **M3's explanation surface** (`crates/yokohama-web/src/explain.rs`, `web/src/Explain.tsx`; spec `docs/plans/2026-08-31-web-frontend-m3-spec.md`): `GET …/suggest` and `GET …/why[?event=n]`, the only two routes that COMPUTE — they read an opinion about a state and never advance one, and they DROP the per-game lock before searching. **A typed refusal is a 200 with a refusal body**, not a 4xx: `why` declining because the last move was a human's is the answer. The DTOs mirror `eval::explain` without touching core; `Feature::name()` is already the stable wire key, the counters are `driven_runner::counter_rows`' fifteen named verbatim, every move is worded by `describe_move`, and `why`'s twelve refusals are `why::account_parts`' own strings carried VERBATIM — **that gate is reached, never reimplemented**. **`suggest`'s four are the one place M3 re-words at all, and the split is not "unwrapped" across the board**: `SuggestRefusal::message`'s doc rules on each — `GameOver` byte-identical to the CLI's line, `LiveMode` the CLI's words hand-wrapped to a view body, and `BotSeat` and `Locked` **AUTHORED** (the CLI's `Locked` line ends in a list of terminal commands that means nothing in a browser). The licence is that those four live inside `repl::suggest_cmd`'s rendered output and no `guide_sync` cell pins them; `Refusal`'s twelve are pinned to the tester guide and so are carried. **The exact-sum identity `total == Σ product + terminal.value` rides the payload and the client foots it on screen.** All 34 terms ship including the zero-product ones, but **NOT because the sum needs them** — a zero product contributes zero, so the carrying terms alone foot (measured on `web/src/wire/suggest.json`: 34 terms, 8 nonzero, both sums 78 == `total`). The reason is `Explanation`'s own and it is about the READER: a term reported at zero lets you see it contributed nothing rather than wonder whether it was computed, and it is what makes the client's "8 of 34" a count of something it is showing. A payload of carrying terms alone could foot; it could not be audited. A TERMINAL explanation is a different picture — every product is 0 by design — so the client draws the standing instead of empty bars. **`ranking[].chosen` is a wire field and not `index == 0`**: ties at the top are broken by a keyed draw, and on the committed fixture two candidates tie at 78 and the played move is row **1**, not row 0, of 186 — a client dropping row 0 to get the runner-ups would list the move the bot played as something it beat. **`why` masks the roads not taken**: at a node where fulfilling is legal the root candidate list enumerates the MOVER's hand (`FulfillOrder`'s only generator reads `order_cards_in_hand`), which the same server serves as `cards: []` — so under `HandVisibility::Masked` an unplayed `FulfillOrder` words as "Fulfill an order from hand" and keeps its row, value and margin. The move that was PLAYED is never masked (it happened, and the card has left the hand), and `suggest` masks nothing — it ranks your own moves from your own view.
  - **The four card indexes and the final score** (`crates/yokohama-web/src/outcome.rs`, `web/src/Index.tsx`): `GET …/index/{orders|techs|goals|sites}[?filter=]` serves `screen_model::build_index` over the WIDENED `Repl::index_placements` — widened rather than copied because its doc defends a leak fence (no other seat's hand). **`target` is always `None`**: the terminal can open an index inside a card capture and number its cards, this API has no capture (the grammar refuses the index words precisely so a digit cannot change meaning), so an index here is a read-only view and numbers nothing. The filter is `card_prompt::parse_filter` — **one card-entry grammar in this project, never a second** — and an unparseable term is a 400 carrying the CLI's own refusal. `GET …/result` is the ranked breakdown, **a 409 while the game is still running**, and a NEW route rather than a widening of `ClientState.scores` (which stays the compact in-frame view its three tests already pin). `Placing.won` is a wire field because **a margin of 0 is not a win** — two seats can tie on points and be separated by the engine's own tie-break.
  - **THE LOCKDOWN RULE (M3 close): the lockdown gate fences COMPUTATION, not PROJECTION.** A read-only GET serves a locked session — M2 settled that for `ports`/`labs`/`areas`/`log`, and `ClientState` reports the lockdown as its prompt — so the card indexes and the final score answer. `/suggest` and `/why` REFUSE, because both re-run the bot against the record the driver declared faulty and an authoritative account of a search over a suspect record is what a lockdown exists to stop. Pinned in both directions and mutation-proven; the asymmetry was a review finding, not a design.
  - **The M3 record is `docs/plans/2026-08-31-web-frontend-m3.md`, and its §9 is the six-lens adversarial review** — 42 findings, 36 reproduced, 5 HIGH, alongside 89 claims verified TRUE. **Read §9.3 before trusting a doc comment in this lane:** five of M3's own sentences were false, in the same vocabulary as M2.5's three, and the method that found them is to try the mechanism the sentence promises rather than to re-read the sentence.   - **Card art** is committed under `crates/yokohama-web/assets/art/`, keyed on the wire's own identifiers: orders by `OrderCardId` (the printed corner index), **techs by TITLE** (inside `rules_hash`; the registry's `codes` slug is hashed nowhere — **do not add a `TechRegistry::code` accessor for this**, it was written, reviewed and reverted), sites by the 0-based `printed_site_index` the CLI's tile also prints, achievements and area tiles by serde name.

- Hidden information — READ THE DISTINCTION, it is load-bearing for Phase 3. **In the engine's REPRESENTATION** exactly one thing is hidden player-to-player: each player's starting order (`starting_order` / `starting_order_hidden`; both dealt cards sit in `orders_removed`; reveals via `extract_order_from_deck`). **From a SEATED PLAYER's view that is NOT the whole hidden set** — a bot handed a raw `GameState` is cheating: the state also stores the exact future `order_deck`/`tech_deck` ORDER and the boxed `orders_removed` pile (at 2P the boxing is largest — the unknown is largest exactly where the first strength milestone is). Those fields are correct for replay/self-play; they are not an observation. **Phase 3.0 built the boundary**: `types/view.rs` + `game/observe.rs` (masks the FIVE-member hidden set — the four above plus `GameConfig.setup_seed`) + `game/belief.rs` (`BeliefSampler`, conservation-consistent determinization) + `game/agent.rs`. **A bot takes a `PlayerView`, never a raw `GameState`.** Masking the seed does not fully close the channel — the public board fingerprints the seed (the seed-oracle residual, closed operationally by full-`u64` recorded seeds; map §8). The live-tracking path treats the seeded deck/removed split as fiction until physical cards are revealed — the same fact from the other side.

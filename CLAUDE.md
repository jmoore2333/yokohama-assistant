## Architecture

Workspace crates: `crates/yokohama-core` (library), `crates/yokohama-cli` (Phase-2 game driver), `crates/yokohama-oracle` (independent rules oracle, Phase 2.9), `crates/yokohama-difftest` (differential harness), and `crates/gen-data` (YAML→Rust data generator tool). The CLI's public modules are `describe`/`render`/`repl`/`screen`/`session`/`style`/`wizard` plus crate-private `input`/`reveal`, over a thin `main` — `screen` composes the whole cleared frame (title, board, players panel, legend, info strip, message zone, prompt) and owns `visible_width`, the escape-stripping measurement every width guard uses; `style` owns ALL ANSI knowledge (`paint(Tone::…, token)`, identity until `main` calls `init_from_tty`, `NO_COLOR` honored) and states the alignment rule callers follow — pad from the PLAIN text, since escapes add bytes with zero display width.

- `types/` — serde-serializable state. `GameState` is complete-and-resumable and **history-free by design** — it must stay cheap to clone for future MCTS; move history belongs in an external action log, never in state. `Move`/`AreaAction` enums in `types/moves.rs`. Typed `MoveError` in `types/errors.rs`.
- `game/` — pure functions over state: `get_legal_moves(&GameState, &Rules) -> Vec<Move>` and `apply_move(&GameState, &Move, &Rules) -> Result<GameState, MoveError>` (clones, never mutates). Since engine-authority (2026-07-19), `apply_move` is the CHECKED boundary — it rejects any move not in the current legal list before applying; `apply_move_unchecked` is the internal fast path (fuzzer/self-play, moves already drawn from the legal list). `Rules` (`game/rules.rs`) bundles static data: `Rules::standard()` from generated registries, `Rules::empty_for_tests()` for fixtures; `Rules::content_hash()` stamps `GameRecord.rules_hash` so a record knows which rules data produced it (`replay` warns on mismatch, `replay_strict` also runs per-event invariant checks — the training-record path). Setup: seeded `new_game(config, rules, seed) -> PendingSetup` + `finalize_setup(pending, choices)`; decks live IN `GameState` (`order_deck`/`tech_deck`, last element = top; `orders_removed` for conservation).
- `game/search_actions.rs` (Phase 3.1a) — the FACTORED bot-facing action source, families-first: `families(state, rules) -> Vec<MoveFamily>` names the families legal at the current decision point, then `family_len`/`iter_family`/`sample_family` page or sample WITHIN one (`sample_family -> Option<Move>`: `None` on a structurally-empty family, e.g. a fully-blocked routes step — `families()` deliberately emits the tag without probing emptiness). **THE THREE FAMILY-COST SHAPES DIFFER, and the difference is the point — never transfer one family's language to another:** *retreat* is LAZY serve-one — `retreat_move`/`retreat_len`/`retreat_iter`/`retreat_sample` construct straight from a mixed-radix index over the ≤17-entry `on_board` map (last area is the least-significant digit, matching `enumerate_recovery_options`), so one candidate off the 442,368-move product costs ~5 allocations and `family_len` is the closed form `∏(count+1)`, exact at any size; *placement* is LAZY generate-and-filter — `place_combo`/`placement_iter`/`placement_sample` build combinations with per-combination affordability, so `family_len` is an honest UPPER bound (a strict over-estimate once a fee gate punches a hole, since affordable subsets are not contiguous in enumeration order — `iter_family` is the authority on the realized moves); *routes* is COMPUTE-PER-CALL — small but exact, one full enumeration per `iter_family`/`sample_family`/`family_len` call with no cache in 3.1b, so a caller needing both count and moves should page the iterator rather than ask twice (ledger L10). The remaining small families delegate to the existing exhaustive generators, so they are equivalent by construction. **`get_legal_moves` stays the authority** — the frozen exhaustive surface `apply_move` checks against, used by CLI/oracle/tests; these modules are purely additive and are what search calls.
- `game/route_enum.rs` (Phase 3.1b) — the exact allocation-free President-route enumerator that retired 3.1a's `MovementRoutes` delegation stub. `enumerate_routes` reproduces the frozen `enumerate_president_routes` EXACTLY (same `(destination, path)` pairs, same emission order, same lex-least witness), and `movement_routes_family` assembles the WHOLE Movement family the way `legal_moves.rs:301-367` does — walked routes, then at most one Station teleport, then Tram teleports ascending, with `zero_cost_destinations` suppression, or the from-hand `PlacePresident` arm when the President is in hand. The win is allocation shape, not complexity: the label is three scalars (`destination`, opponent-owner bitmask, canal-toll count — `route_fee_vector` is a closed form and each owner contributes 0 or 1 on a simple path), so the DFS carries `(u8, u32)` over fixed arrays and never builds a `RouteVector` during search; worst-case TIME is still `O(affordable simple paths)` for both sides and nothing here claims otherwise. Measured at HEAD: the 458-label dense cell costs 467 allocations against the frozen reference's 1,687,537 (3,613×). Totality is a PRE-FLIGHT probe — `fits_fixed_arrays` checks all four bounds (areas ≤ 18, out-degree ≤ 17, label classes, opponents ≤ 3) BEFORE any array write, and a state that fails it (only hand-built fixtures can) delegates to the frozen path rather than panicking. The routes family is SMALL — max OBSERVED 694 adversarial / 38 organic under one stated uniform-random policy, and **no label ceiling is known** (ledger L9): every citation of those numbers reads "max observed under harness X", never a bound.
- `game/move_legality.rs` (Phase 3.1b) — single-candidate legality for a search that PROPOSES a move: `is_move_legal(state, mv, rules, mode)` (exhaustive match over `Move`, no `_` arm, so a new variant is a compile error rather than a silent rejection) and `is_president_path_legal`. **Design A** — re-state the cheap per-arm predicates over `&GameState` — won open decision #3 by measurement over design B (`apply_move_unchecked(…).is_ok()`), which is 2.5–326× slower on a whole-`GameState`-clone floor AND unsound alone: the raw arms deliberately defend only structure, so they accept Building without power 4+, unreachable movement, `PlacePresident` onto a non-Possible-Action Area, and every wrong-PHASE move (no raw arm reads `turn_phase` at all). `PathMode::RulesLegal` accepts any rules-legal WITNESS and is deliberately WIDER than list membership — a non-canonical path for a label the generator emits is an ACCEPT, with ZERO new labels, so equivalence tests compare `MovePresident` by `(destination, RouteVector)` LABEL and never by raw path; `PathMode::Canonical` adds back only the generator's teleport suppression and explicitly does NOT verify lex-least witness selection (that needs the enumerator). The module has exactly ONE narrowing vs `get_legal_moves`, documented at the arm: on a fixture-only state (own assistant on the Canal) the generator emits `PlacePresident{Canal}` while both apply paths reject it — a pre-existing generator/apply inconsistency in the frozen files, unreachable by legal play, and siding with apply is what keeps accepts⟹apply-Ok true.
- A turn is a phase state machine (`TurnPhase`): AdditionalActionFirst → Placement → Movement → AreaAction → FivePowerBonus → Building → Recovery (automatic) → AdditionalActionSecond → TurnEnd. Mercenary use runs a nested sub-turn (AreaAction→…→Recovery) — see `is_mercenary_action`.
- Hidden information — READ THE DISTINCTION, it is load-bearing for Phase 3. **In the engine's REPRESENTATION** exactly one thing is hidden player-to-player: each player's starting order (`starting_order`, or `starting_order_hidden` for live-tracked opponents — both dealt cards then sit in `orders_removed`; reveals use `extract_order_from_deck`, which searches deck then removed pile). **From a SEATED PLAYER's view that is NOT the whole hidden set**, and a bot handed a raw `GameState` is therefore cheating: `GameState` also stores the exact future `order_deck` / `tech_deck` ORDER and the `orders_removed` boxed pile, neither of which a real player can inspect (at 2P the boxing is largest, so the unknown is largest exactly where the first strength milestone is). Those fields are correct for deterministic replay and self-play; they are not an observation. A Phase-3 player needs an explicit seat-relative observation boundary plus a sampler over the unobserved remainder — never raw `GameState`. (The live-tracking path already treats the seeded deck/removed split as fiction until physical cards are revealed, which is the same fact from the other side.) **Phase 3.0 built exactly that boundary** (branch `phase3.0-observation-belief`): `types/view.rs` (`PlayerView`/`HandCard`/`ViewConfig`, pure data) + `game/observe.rs` (`observe`, masks the FIVE-member hidden set) + `game/belief.rs` (`BeliefSampler`, conservation-consistent determinization back to full `GameState`s) + `game/agent.rs` (`HonestAgent`/`TrivialHonestAgent`/`run_honest_game`) are the observation surface — a bot takes a `PlayerView`, never a raw `GameState`. The hidden set is FIVE members (the four above plus `GameConfig.setup_seed`); masking the seed field does not fully close the channel because the public board fingerprints the seed (seed-oracle residual, closed operationally by full-`u64` recorded seeds at 3.2 — see the map §8 ledger and guidingpath's 2026-07-25 rows).
- Static card data: source of truth is `analysis-1/yokohama-kb/07..09-*.yaml` (**verified 2026-07-17**: 36 orders keyed by printed corner index, 20 tech titles × `copies:` = 30 physical cards, 24 sites with TL/TR/ML/MR `SpaceReward` spaces). `gen-data` emits `game/data.rs`.
- Boards (verified from board art 2026-07-17): port boards have SIX card slots (2 per LOW/MID/HIGH zone), lab boards SIX tech slots (surcharges +1,+1,+2,+2 on slots 3–6), customs board 7 spaces with printed costs [1,1,2,2,3,3,4], church board faith 4–11.
- Area-board topology (`board_gen`, corrected 2026-07-21, SEMANTICS_REV 2): a STAGGERED PYRAMID, widest row on top — rows 4/3/2/1 (2P) · 5/4/3/2 (3P) · 6/5/4/3 (4P), brick-offset adjacency (a tile touches up to SIX neighbors), undirected edge counts pinned at 18/28/38, any-to-any Canal. NOT a rectangular grid (the pre-2026-07-21 model was 2×5/2×7/3×6 orthogonal-only and missed ~30% of real edges). `BoardLayout.adjacency` is the single graph all consumers read, and there is no row-metadata field on `GameState`. The two renderers get their rows differently, deliberately: `render::board_grid` (the LIVE board) groups tiles purely by `AreaConfig::grid_position` and **assumes no shape at all** — "any geometry the engine (or a test fixture) hands us renders" — so it cannot drift from the data; `board_gen::row_lengths(pc)` is used only where there is no state to read, by `render::board_skeleton` (the empty numbered board the live wizard shows before setup) and by `wizard`'s tile-entry prompts, pinned by `board_skeleton_shape_matches_row_lengths_at_every_player_count`.

## Rules knowledge

- Complete reference: `analysis-1/yokohama-kb/01-rules-reference.md` (being corrected as board-art verification finds errors — the decision log lists corrections); rulebook PDF in `analysis-1/`; icon glossary is rulebook p.10.
- Commonly-missed rules that have bitten this project: Trading House passive income (owner gets 1 Yen when another player acts in that area); movement-icon rewards move *assistants* via queued declinable choices — there is NO 5-Power "Movement token" (box-verified 2026-07-18: no Movement or VP faces exist in the 20-token pool); mercenaries bypass opponent Presidents (no blocking, no fees); the President may not return to its starting area in the same turn; placement fees are 1 Yen PER ASSISTANT PLACED to each president owner (Option B's 2-into-one-area costs 2, not 1); movement pass-through fees are paid TO the president owners (1 Yen each), not the bank; Trading House and Shophouse both require Power 4+ (no separate higher gate for Trading House — corrected 2026-07-17, final-review finding C3); a player may only own 1 Technology card of the same name (10 of the 20 titles circulate as 2 physical copies so different players can each own one); trading-house tokens go warehouse→hand at the Employment Agency (printed 4/5/6/7 take progression) and build FREE from hand; port refill is rightmost-first after total left-compaction; Port at 5 Power also grants +3 VP; Chinatown exchange count is capped by action power; Movement Option B is legal with the President already in hand; the 2P dummy pawn (church faith-7 / customs space-4) is a real scoring occupant that COMPETES in the church (6/3) and customs (8/4) majority bonuses — not merely an eligibility block (finding D1, rulebook p.9 NOTE — **Jeremy's edition only**, NOT the English PDF bundled in `analysis-1/`; the authoritative text lives in guidingpath's 2026-07-21 decision-log rows and `docs/plans/2026-07-20-phase-2.9-ruling-list.md` §D1 — modeled 2026-07-21).
- Church faith = power LINEARLY (1,2,3,4,5) plus 1 per distinct payment type (max 1 each of copper/silk/tea/seafood/yen/import). The rulebook text's "2 Power: 1 Faith" is a TYPO (Jeremy-adjudicated 2026-07-17); the engine's `calculate_church_faith` is corrected in Task 13.
- Choice encoding LANDED (2026-07-19) — the former v1 auto-resolutions are real queued choices: any-good picks (no decline — gain is unconditional), warehouse-icon takes (Decline always offered; priced per the 4/5/6/7 progression), movement-icon assistant moves (Decline always offered per icon), church/customs space choice (queued only when ≥2 spaces eligible — exactly one place inline), Telephone's Recovery leave-one (may keep a LONE assistant — recovers zero at count 1), Station placement (7th variant, engine-authority — every non-Canal area is eligible), University/Spinning Mill as standing additional-action options. Singleton resolution sets auto-resolve silently. For every DECLINABLE choice that singleton is structurally a no-op Decline (pinned invariant — CLI delta windows depend on it); `StationPlace` is the documented exception — it has no Decline at all, so a lone eligible area places INLINE with a real effect (design-sanctioned, unreachable on any generated board, pinned by `choices.rs`'s `station_place_with_single_placeable_area_places_inline_no_choice_event` and scoped in `choice_encoding.rs`'s `assert_decline_or_never_singleton`).
- Canal (re-ruled 2026-07-21 ANY-TO-ANY on the staggered board — supersedes the 2026-07-18/19 colinear-through-lines model, which was reasoned on the old rectangular grid): the Canal connects ANY of its adjacent tiles to ANY other for President movement; 1 Yen per crossing to the BANK (not a player). The tolled `canal_crossings` SET is only the neighbor pairs NOT already joined by a free brick-offset edge — a pair that IS directly adjacent keeps its free edge and the free step governs (the tolled transit there is legal but dominated, self-resolving), so every neighbor pair still ends up connected in `adjacency` (freely or via a toll). The Canal itself is never placeable/stoppable and Telegram's adjacent-bonus placement excludes crossing pairs (transit-only adjacency). Derivation single-sourced in `board_gen::derive_canal_crossings`, reused by `reconcile_board`.
